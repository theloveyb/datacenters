## Major Investments

### Microsoft — C$19 Billion (2023–2027)
- Largest investment in Microsoft Canada's 40-year history.
- C$7.5 B earmarked for 2026–2027 specifically for Azure data-center expansion.
- Primary regions: **Toronto (Canada Central)** and **Quebec City (Canada East)**.
- New capacity begins coming online H2 2026.
- Announced Dec 2025, includes GPU additions for AI workloads and a digital-sovereignty plan.

### Federal Government Programs
- **Budget 2024**: C$2.4 B for AI computing infrastructure and safety research.
- **Fall Economic Statement 2024**: Up to C$15 B in aggregate loan/equity investments for AI DC projects co-invested by Canadian pension funds (2:1 pension-to-government ratio).
- **Cohere / Bell AI Fabric**: C$240 M federal commitment; 500 MW full-build target.

### CPP Investments — Cambridge, ON
- C$225 M construction financing (50% interest alongside Deutsche Bank).
- 54 MW hyperscale expansion, pre-leased to a GPU-focused AI cloud provider.

### QScale — Greater Toronto Area
- Multi-billion-dollar AI hyperscale facility planned near Toronto.
- Received preliminary Hydro One validation for power.
- Currently finishing Q01 Phase 3 in Lévis, QC (142 MW, C$1.2 B).

## Ontario Power Grid Constraints

- **6,500 MW** of proposed data centers have applied for Ontario grid access — roughly **30% of Ontario's 2024 peak demand**.
- The IESO projects summer peak demand will exceed dependable capacity by ~2,500 MW as early as **2028**.
- Connection Assessment & Approval (CAA) timelines: 11–13 months to conditional approval, then multiple years for transmission work.
- Total lead times for 100+ MW loads in constrained areas can stretch to **5+ years**.

### Bill 40 (2025)
Ontario introduced legislation requiring data centers to obtain **Ministerial approval** before grid connection, prioritizing projects that maximize economic benefit and job creation.

### Ontario's Nuclear Advantage
Ontario's electricity mix is ~58% nuclear, ~24% hydro — one of the lowest-carbon grids globally. This is a significant draw for hyperscalers facing ESG scrutiny, differentiating Ontario from gas-dependent provinces like Alberta.

---

## Competitive Context

- Toronto dominates the Canadian DC market (86 facilities vs Montreal's 57).
- Ontario + Quebec jointly hold ~45% of Canada's national DC market share.
- **Alberta** is seeing explosive DC growth: grid-connection requests surged from 200 MW (Q1 2024) to 11,879 MW (Q1 2025) — a 5,800% increase — but the grid is gas-dependent.
- **Global context**: Hyperscaler capex (Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta) reached nearly $200 B globally in 2024, expected to grow 40%+ in 2025.
- Canada-wide DC market projected to reach $12.27 B USD investment by 2030.
